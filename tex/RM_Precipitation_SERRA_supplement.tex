\section{Robustness of the event-based scoring}\label{sec:matching}
All analyses in this section are post hoc (protocol Amendments~2 and~3, Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) and
are computed by \texttt{code/serra/matching\_checks.py} and \texttt{code/serra/review\_checks.py} from the
stored detector scores, at the thresholds calibrated for the primary budget. With the pre-specified rule, window and year resampling the script
reproduces the tables of the main paper exactly.

\paragraph{Matching rule.} The pre-specified rule credits an event with a hit when any alarm falls within
$[-60,+120]$ days of its onset. It does not force a one-to-one correspondence: when the windows of two
events at a station overlap, one alarm can be credited to both, and an alarm that follows the end of a
short event but precedes onset $+120$ days counts both as a late hit and as a false alarm.
Table~\ref{tab:episodes} shows how often this happens. Under the \emph{one-to-one} rule every alarm is
assigned to at most one event: events are processed in chronological order and each takes the earliest
unassigned alarm in its window, which for windows of equal length maximizes the number of matched events;
a false alarm is then an unmatched alarm outside $[\text{onset}-\text{pre},\text{end}]$ of every event.

\paragraph{Scoring window.} Both rules are re-scored with pre-onset limits of 60, 30 and 0 days and
post-onset limits of 120, 90 and 60 days (Tables~\ref{tab:matching} and~\ref{tab:matchingb}; the
temperature-aware analysis is in Table~\ref{tab:matchingmv}). The false-alarm window moves with the
pre-onset limit.

\paragraph{Random-timing reference.} With one alarm per station-year and a 180-day window, alarms that carry
no information still score hits. The reference is the POD obtained when each station's alarm sequence is
shifted circularly within 2001--2024 by a random offset (500 replicates). The shift keeps the number of
alarms and their spacing, including the refractory period, and removes their alignment with the events.
The mean over replicates is reported in Tables~\ref{M-tab:warning} and~\ref{M-tab:warningmv} of the main
paper and, for each window, in Tables~\ref{tab:matching} and~\ref{tab:matchingmv}.

\paragraph{Dependence.} Station-level onsets in a region are linked into one episode when at most 30 days
apart (Table~\ref{tab:episodes}). Table~\ref{tab:blockboot} repeats the paired intervals with circular
blocks of two, three and four consecutive years, which allows for dependence between successive years.

\paragraph{Monthly SPI-3 rule.} The alarm count of the month-end down-crossing rule (D4) is not monotone
in its threshold $c$. In Germany and Australia at the primary budget, and in Germany and Taiwan at 0.5
alarms per station-year, the pre-specified grid search met the budget with a positive threshold, at which
the rule alarms on returns from wet conditions; the dry-side threshold meets the budget equally well and
gives a much higher POD. The tables of the main paper and Tables~\ref{tab:warning05}
and~\ref{tab:warning2} therefore use the threshold restricted to $c\le0$, as a post hoc correction, and
Table~\ref{tab:spithree} gives the pre-specified calibration beside it. At two alarms per station-year
the rule cannot attain the budget with either sign, so the comparison with the defect at that budget is
not at a matched alarm rate. Fig.~4 of the main paper shows SPI-3 for dry-side thresholds only.

\paragraph{Detection by deadline.} Timing compared on events that two detectors both detect conditions on
joint detection. The unconditional summary is the fraction of all reference onsets for which a detector
has alarmed by day $d$ relative to the onset, counting alarms from day $-60$ on, so that a missed onset
never counts (Tables~\ref{tab:deadline} and~\ref{tab:deadlinediff}; Fig.~\ref{fig:deadlineregions} by
region; pooled curves in Fig.~\ref{M-fig:deadline} of the main paper).

\paragraph{Climatological reference of the event definition.} The pre-specified events use day-of-year
soil-wetness percentiles computed over all years 1981--2024, so the dating of an onset in an early
evaluation year depends on later years. Table~\ref{tab:fixedref} repeats the main comparisons with the
percentile reference fixed to 1981--2000 and applied unchanged to 2001--2024; events are re-derived by the
same run rules. Detector thresholds are unchanged, because calibration does not use the events. The fixed
reference gives more onsets in 2001--2024, since the later years are drier relative to 1981--2000 than
relative to the whole record. The forward-chaining analysis of Section~\ref{sec:forward} is repeated
with labels from these events, so that neither the model nor the label of a training week uses later
years.

\paragraph{Illustrative station traces.} Fig.~\ref{fig:traces} shows the droughts cited in
Section~\ref{M-sec:events} of the main paper at one station each, chosen for illustration rather than as a
test. In the Texas, German and Australian cases the defect alarmed \traceDonelaglo--\traceDonelaghi\ days
after the reference onset and \traceDthreeminusDonelo--\traceDthreeminusDonehi\ days before SPI-90, and
SPI-30 alarmed \traceDsevenminusDonelo--\traceDsevenminusDonehi\ days after the defect. In the Taiwan case
the defect did not alarm, whereas SPI-30 alarmed \traceDseventaiwan\ day before the onset and SPI-90
\traceDthreetaiwan\ days after it.

\section{Validation schemes for the incremental-information analysis}\label{sec:forward}
The pre-specified scheme scores the weekly logistic models by five-fold cross-validation over contiguous
blocks of years, so that the training folds include years after the test block.
Table~\ref{tab:forward} compares it with schemes in which a week is predicted only from earlier weeks
(\texttt{code/serra/incremental\_forward.py}; post hoc). In forward chaining, the weeks of year $Y$ in
2001--2024 are predicted by a model fitted to the weeks since 1982 that precede 1 January of year $Y$ by
more than 60 days, the prediction horizon, so that every training label was known before the year began;
the model is refitted every year, or every six years in a variant. A further variant keeps the blocked
folds and removes training weeks within 60 days of a test block on either side. The predictors are
prospective in every scheme, because the networks and all reference distributions are fitted on
1961--1990. By-region results under annual forward chaining are in Tables~\ref{M-tab:incremental}
and~\ref{M-tab:incrementalmv} of the main paper.

\section{Forecast equivalence under year-cluster resampling}\label{sec:forecastcluster}
The equivalence tests of the main paper use $t$ intervals over stations, which treat stations as
independent. Table~\ref{tab:forecastcluster} adds percentile intervals obtained by resampling the 24
calendar years of 2001--2024 within each region and recomputing every station's mean squared error on the
resampled years, which keeps the dependence among stations that share weather (post hoc;
\texttt{code/serra/review\_checks.py}). The conclusions are the same: equivalence within $\pm2\%$ in
every case except the temperature-aware network against the GRU in Australia.

\section{Code, data and reproduction}\label{sec:code}
Online Resource 2 holds the scripts in \texttt{code/}, the protocol, the result files in
\texttt{results/} and a \texttt{README.md} with the exact commands. The steps are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{fetch\_ghcnd\_long.py}, \texttt{build\_taiwan.py} (with the archived
\texttt{fetch\_taiwan\_hydromet.py}) and \texttt{fetch\_power.py} download and assemble the gauge records
and the MERRA-2 covariates and apply the completeness rules.
\item \texttt{serra/train\_models.py} trains the regularized network, its $\lambda=0$ ablation and the
GRU (five seeds per station) and stores daily forecasts, defects and path increments.
\item \texttt{serra/build\_scores.py} computes the detector scores, SPI-90, SPI-3, soil-moisture
percentiles and reference events; \texttt{serra/add\_spi30.py} adds the post hoc SPI-30.
\item \texttt{serra/evaluate\_warning.py}, \texttt{serra/forecast\_eval.py} and
\texttt{serra/covariate\_analysis.py} produce the pre-specified results; \texttt{build\_temperature.py},
\texttt{serra/train\_models\_mv.py}, \texttt{serra/build\_scores\_mv.py}, \texttt{serra/covariate\_analysis\_mv.py}
and \texttt{serra/drift\_mv.py} (with the \texttt{--mv} option of the evaluation scripts) carry out the
temperature-aware analysis.
\item \texttt{serra/matching\_checks.py}, \texttt{serra/incremental\_forward.py} and
\texttt{serra/review\_checks.py} (each also with \texttt{--mv}) produce the post hoc checks of
Sections~\ref{sec:matching}--\ref{sec:forecastcluster}; \texttt{serra/extract\_intermediates.py} writes the
small intermediate file they need (raw soil wetness and station-year forecast errors).
\item \texttt{serra/text\_checks.py} tabulates the descriptive quantities quoted for the figures
(composites, operating curves, station traces) and the interval-width comparison;
\texttt{serra/make\_tables.py} writes every table and every number quoted in the text, and stops if a
descriptive statement no longer matches the results; \texttt{serra/figures.py} draws the figures.
\item \texttt{serra/verify\_from\_csv.py} rebuilds every table and every number quoted in the paper from
the result files in the archive and compares them with the published ones. It needs none of the omitted
files and is the default stage of \texttt{reproduce.sh}.
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Random seeds.} Network training uses seed $1000k+s$ for seed index $k=0,\dots,4$, where $s$ is
the integer formed by the last four characters of the station identifier (0 if they are not digits). The
bootstrap seeds are fixed in the scripts (20261112 for the warning evaluation and its robustness checks;
7 and 11 for the incremental-information analyses; 5 for the drift check; offsets of these for the post
hoc checks, as listed in \texttt{README.md}).

\paragraph{Software versions.} The analyses were run with Python~3.12 and the package versions in
\texttt{requirements.txt} (PyTorch~2.3, NumPy~1.26, SciPy~1.13, pandas~2.2, scikit-learn~1.9,
statsmodels~0.14, Numba~0.60, Matplotlib~3.10, or later). The post hoc checks, and an independent re-run of the warning evaluation and
of the covariate analyses from the stored detector scores, used Python~3.13, NumPy~2.5, SciPy~1.18,
pandas~3.0, scikit-learn~1.9, statsmodels~0.15 and Numba~0.68; the warning evaluation reproduced the stored
results exactly and the covariate analyses to within $2\times10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{What is and is not in the archive.} Online Resource 2 contains the code, the protocol, the
station lists, all result tables, the reference events, the intermediate file of
\texttt{extract\_intermediates.py} and the figures. With these, every table and number of the paper can be
rebuilt and checked (\texttt{verify\_from\_csv.py}). Re-running the post hoc checks and redrawing the
composite and trace figures needs the daily score panels (about 200~MB), and the full pipeline needs the
raw downloads and the per-seed score files (about 2~GB), which the scripts rebuild; the score panels are
available from the authors and will be deposited with the public release. Training all models took about
one hour (precipitation-only) and one hour (temperature-aware) on a 16-core workstation.
